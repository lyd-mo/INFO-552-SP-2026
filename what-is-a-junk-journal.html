What is a Junk Journal?

Definition

Great question! We think Justine Jenkins of House of Mahalo describes it best:

"A junk journal is a book that's made out of found and recycled materials, such as trash, packaging, paper scraps, book pages, music sheets, maps, illustrations, and ephemera such as stamps, ticket stubs, postcards, greeting cards, fussy cut pictures, etc. It is a place where you can record your thoughts, ideas, memories, and musings." (from House of Mahalo)

When it comes to junk journaling, there are no rules. You can use any materials you find and let your creativity guide you. Some people even make the actual journal out of junk itself!

Where did junk journaling come from?

Junk journaling has its roots in the broader craft movement and the growing interest in sustainable and eco-friendly practices. It draws inspiration from various art forms and DIY culture, where creativity is encouraged and materials are repurposed.

How is it different from scrapbooking?

Junk journaling is distinct from scrapbooking in that it emphasizes the use of items that are typically discarded or considered "junk."

Scrapbooking, on the other hand, often involves using more polished, purchased, and decorative materials to create a visually appealing layout.

Junk journaling encourages creativity and self-expression through the use of unconventional materials, while scrapbooking focuses more on preserving memories in a visually organized manner.

Additionally, junk journalling is more affordable and accessible than scrapbooking, as it utilizes materials that are often free or low-cost. Scrapbooking can be quite expensive, especially when using specialty materials and embellishments.

What Do You Need to Get Started?

To get started with junk journaling, you'll need a few basic supplies:

  • A blank book or journal
  • Scissors
  • Glue or tape
  • Found and recycled materials (junk!)
  • Optional: pens, markers, paint, stamps, stickers, etc. for decorating
  • Your imagination!
